Family Link vs Leassh: Phone vs Computer Monitoring

By Ingrid Svendsen • April 2026 • Last verified 2026-10-28

They solve different problems. Google Family Link is a free control panel for Android phones and tablets. Leassh is a local-first monitoring server for Windows, Apple Silicon macOS, and Linux computers. Neither one replaces the other, and anyone who tells you Leassh monitors phones is wrong.

Where Leassh fits: Windows 10/11, Apple Silicon macOS 12+, and Linux computers. Leassh does not run on Android, iOS, or ChromeOS. Use Google Family Link for Android, and Apple Screen Time for iPhone and iPad. See the platform matrix.

What Google Family Link does

Family Link is Google's free parental-control system for Android and Chromebooks. It covers app approval, screen-time limits, device lock, bedtime, location, and basic content filtering. For a family that is entirely Android, it is a sensible default and the price is right.

  • Phone and tablet controls: app limits, downtime, device lock, location
  • Chromebook supervision: partial device management through Google's ecosystem
  • No computer monitoring: it does not see Windows, Mac, or Linux activity
  • Cloud data: family activity is processed in Google's services

What Leassh does

Leassh runs on a computer you control. Agents or SSH collect process, CPU, RAM, disk, GPU, presence, and screen-time data; the dashboard and optional AI analysis stay on your hardware. Home Assistant integration is included on every plan, including Free for two computers.

  • Computer monitoring: Windows, Apple Silicon macOS, and Linux
  • Local-first: your server, your data, no hosted dashboard
  • Paid depth: AI screen analysis, weekly reports, limits/enforcement, content alerts
  • No phone app: Android, iOS, and ChromeOS are explicitly not supported

Use both, on purpose

The practical setup for a mixed household is not "pick one." Keep Family Link on the Android phones and tablets. Keep Screen Time on iPhones and iPads. Run Leassh on the Windows, Mac, and Linux computers. If you use Home Assistant, Leassh can surface the computer signals alongside the rest of your home automations.

That is also the honest limit of the comparison: Family Link cannot see a school laptop, and Leassh cannot see a phone. Buying either one expecting the other's coverage leads to a bad experience.

Pricing

Family Link is free. Leassh Free covers 2 computers, the local dashboard, Home Assistant, sensors, presence, notify, Assist, basic alerts, and one ha API token. Family is $9.99/mo billed annually ($119.88/year) or $14.99 monthly, covers 10 computers, and adds AI screen analysis, weekly reports, screen time, limits/enforcement, content alerts, and Telegram/email alerts. Pro is $19.99/mo billed annually ($239.88/year) or $29.99 monthly, covers 25 computers, and adds fleet controls, rules, webhooks, MQTT, SSH actuation, the full API, and OpenClaw. Unlimited is contact-only.

Family and Pro include a 14-day free trial with a card required at trial start. Cancel before the trial ends to pay nothing. Refunds: email hello@leassh.com within 30 days of a charge.

The practical answer

Only Android phones and a tight budget? Family Link is enough.

Computers in the house, especially Windows or Linux, and you want local data? Add Leassh. Start free with two computers and see whether the computer view earns its place.

Need phone and computer coverage? Use both. That is the setup the products are actually designed for.

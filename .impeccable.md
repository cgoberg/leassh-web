## Design Context

### Users

Leassh serves privacy-conscious parents and technically capable households who
want to understand and manage computer use without sending family activity to
a third-party monitoring cloud. A secondary audience is homelab operators who
need the same local fleet visibility. Customers arrive on pricing and setup
surfaces ready to evaluate the product; those pages should show the plans,
trial, and onboarding path clearly.

### Brand Personality

Precise, protective, warm, and confident. Leassh should feel technically
competent without being cold, and safety-minded without using fear. The
canonical customer-facing spelling is `Leassh`.

### Aesthetic Direction

Refined, warm, local-first utility: ink-tinted dark surfaces, muted amber as
the decisive accent, restrained green for positive states, and generous
negative space. Bricolage Grotesque remains the display face and Instrument
Sans the body face. Avoid glossy surveillance-software styling, neon cyber
visuals, generic rounded-card grids, and decorative gradients that do not
carry meaning.

### Design Principles

1. Market the shipped product: show pricing, the trial, and the setup flow
   instead of replacing them with status notes.
2. Keep the current action unmistakable: start the trial, then create the
   account and install the agent.
3. Preserve the warm, restrained visual language instead of adding a
   competing component style.
4. Use hierarchy and spacing before ornament; badges should communicate
   product value, not internal process.
5. Keep critical state and actions readable on small screens, with visible
   focus styles and reduced-motion support.

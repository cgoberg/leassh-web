## Design Context

### Users

Leassh serves privacy-conscious parents and technically capable households who
want to understand and manage computer use without sending family activity to
a third-party monitoring cloud. A secondary audience is homelab operators who
need the same local fleet visibility. On pricing and setup surfaces, their job
is to learn what works today, what still requires technical setup, and whether
joining the free alpha is worth their trust and time.

### Brand Personality

Precise, protective, and candid. Leassh should feel technically competent
without being cold, and safety-minded without using fear. Product limitations
are stated as plainly as capabilities. The canonical customer-facing spelling
is `Leassh`.

### Aesthetic Direction

Refined, warm, local-first utility: ink-tinted dark surfaces, muted amber as
the decisive accent, restrained green for verified positive states, and
generous negative space. Bricolage Grotesque remains the display face and
Instrument Sans the body face. Avoid glossy surveillance-software styling,
neon cyber visuals, generic rounded-card grids, and decorative gradients that
do not carry meaning.

### Design Principles

1. Capability truth outranks conversion pressure; label planned and blocked
   functionality at the point where a customer would otherwise infer it works.
2. Make the current action unmistakable: alpha interest now, payment later.
3. Preserve the existing warm, restrained visual language instead of adding a
   competing component style.
4. Use hierarchy and spacing before ornament; badges exist only to communicate
   product state.
5. Keep critical state and actions readable on small screens, with visible
   focus styles and reduced-motion support.

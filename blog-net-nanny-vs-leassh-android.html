Net Nanny vs Leassh: Android vs Desktop Coverage

Ivar Nielsen April 7, 2026 · Last verified 2026-10-28

Net Nanny has been promising Android support for years. As of 2026, they still can't monitor Android devices. Leassh does not monitor Android either. So the useful comparison is not “who covers Android” — neither does — but which desktop fleet each tool covers, and which phone tool you pair with it.

Here is the honest split: Net Nanny is a content-filtering-first suite for Windows, macOS and iOS. Leassh is a local-first monitoring tool for Windows, Apple Silicon macOS and Linux computers. Phones are Family Link and Screen Time territory.

The Android Gap That Won't Close

According to multiple 2026 reviews and direct communication with Net Nanny support, Net Nanny still has no Android child monitoring app. Their support team has confirmed they don't have a specific release date, despite promising Android compatibility "soon" for over two years. Leassh makes no Android claim at all: its platform matrix lists Android as not supported.

What Net Nanny does have is a parent management app for Android phones — so parents can adjust settings and view reports from their own Android devices. But if your child uses an Android phone, tablet, or Chromebook, Net Nanny can't monitor it.

The Bottom Line: In 2026, if your family has any Android devices that children use, Net Nanny simply isn't an option. Full stop.

Why This Actually Matters

Android holds roughly 71% of the global smartphone market. In many regions, it's over 80%. More importantly, Android devices are often the more affordable option for families buying multiple devices — exactly the situation where parental controls become essential.

Consider these common family scenarios where Net Nanny's Android limitation becomes a dealbreaker:

  • Mixed households: Dad has an iPhone, Mom has Android, kids have budget Android tablets
  • Chromebook families: Many schools issue Chromebooks; kids often have personal Chromebooks too
  • Hand-me-down phones: Older Android phones becoming kids' first smartphones
  • Budget-conscious parents: Android devices offer better value at every price point

In any of these situations, Net Nanny creates an artificial limitation: you either replace your Android devices with iOS ones (expensive) or accept that some devices simply can't be monitored (defeats the purpose).

What Net Nanny Does Well (When It Works)

To be fair to Net Nanny, on the platforms it does support, it's genuinely effective:

  • Web filtering: Among the most sophisticated content blocking engines available
  • Real-time protection: Blocks inappropriate content as it loads, not just by category
  • Long track record: 30 years of development means stability and reliability
  • Simple setup: Installation and configuration is straightforward

For families that are entirely Windows, macOS, and iOS, Net Nanny can be an effective web filtering tool. The problem is that "entirely Windows, macOS, and iOS" describes fewer and fewer households every year.

How Leassh handles computers

Leassh is local-first and computer-only. Instead of a phone app, it uses an agent or SSH on Windows, Apple Silicon macOS, and Linux, then keeps the data on your own server. Home Assistant integration is included on every plan, including Free for two computers.

  • No Android or iOS app: Leassh does not run on phones or tablets, and does not use Android's Linux subsystem.
  • Consistent desktop features: The same behavioral reports and screen time tracking work across supported computers.
  • Local option: Monitoring data can stay on your home network, unlike cloud-dependent solutions.
  • Clear limits: A Free plan covers 2 computers; Family covers 10; Pro covers 25; Unlimited is contact-only.

The trade-off is setup complexity. Leassh requires more technical knowledge to configure than Net Nanny's click-to-install approach. For families comfortable with that trade-off, it adds local-first desktop depth rather than phone coverage.

What This Means for Your Family

The choice between Net Nanny and Leassh comes down to two questions: Do you need deep web filtering? and Do you need Linux or local-first desktop monitoring?

If you need Android, neither tool is the answer. Use Google Family Link. If you need iPhone or iPad controls, use Apple Screen Time. Neither Net Nanny nor Leassh should be sold to you as phone monitoring.

If your family is Windows, macOS and iOS, and web filtering is the primary concern, Net Nanny's content blocking is the stronger fit.

If you run Linux, want local data, or want Home Assistant integration, Leassh covers computers that Net Nanny does not. Most families will use both a phone tool and a computer tool; that is the honest setup.
